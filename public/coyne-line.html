Handicapper's Read

Why the line sits above the plan. The race plan projects 4:02. A book prices the distribution, not the plan, and first-marathon outcomes are lopsided: the upside caps around five minutes, the downside tail runs past twenty. Runners lose time after mile 20. They almost never find it.

Supporting the under. Twenty miles at 3% decoupling. Goal pace sitting at 75% of max heart rate, confirmed twice in taper. Every variable rehearsed — breakfast, gels, drink, belt, shoes, and a full long run in the rain. Logistics failures wreck most first-timers and that risk is priced out.

Supporting the over. Peak week of 32 miles, so glycogen depth is thin. Miles 20 to 26.2 have zero data behind them. A 60° overnight low means a muggy start with limited evaporative cooling, and clear skies put full sun on the exposed Michigan Avenue stretch from mile 22 to 25.

The whole bet, in one line. Three straight marathon-pace workouts came in 8 to 12 seconds per mile faster than prescribed. That drift is free over two miles and ruinous over twenty. Under 4:03:30 is live — it just has to be earned before mile 13, not after mile 20.
